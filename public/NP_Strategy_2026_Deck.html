Current Gap

Where We Are Today

Today, we serve two distinct builders — and both are under-penetrated.
One blended narrative for two very different buyers
The product engineer persona and the PM/Marketer/Growth persona are treated as one. Engineers buy on SDK quality, monitoring, and release management. PMs and marketers buy on insight speed, no-code leverage, and acting without a ticket.
Product engineers drive adoption but have no explicit motion
Engineers create 53% of Feature Experiments, yet Feature Experiment attach sits at 18.9% despite $33.8M ARR and 34% YoY growth. We have no dedicated engineering GTM motion and no narrative that speaks to how engineers actually buy.
The PM/Marketer/Growth loop needs connective tissue
SR at $9.6M, Web XP at $5M, Guides & Surveys at $12.8M. AI Feedback at 4% source connection rate. AI Assistant launches April 7. Orchestration (Q3) is the connective tissue. The products are solid individually — the opportunity is routing signal between them.
Enterprise expansion routes through the data team — and stalls
Without an engineering motion, every large contract eventually routes through warehouse-native incumbents. Engineering buyers route around the data team, carry larger budgets, and use flags on a daily basis — creating high-frequency use cases we're not capturing.

Product Engineer Bet

Winning the Product Engineer

H1 2026 · Critical Window
The Target
The product engineer — not DevOps, not the SRE
The person who owns a feature end-to-end: spec → flag → experiment → outcome. Their primary output is the code. They evaluate tools on SDK quality, time-to-first-flag, stats rigor, and whether results are interpretable without a data scientist.
The Wedge
Feature Flags is the entry product. Analytics is the unfair advantage.
An engineer installs the SDK to control a rollout — no analytics relationship required, no sales conversation. From there, Feature Experiment follows naturally: "want to measure this?" No separate measurement system needed because Amplitude is already there.
The Timing
A strategic shift is putting engineers in active re-evaluation — right now.
Recent consolidation among the leading independent flag and experimentation platforms has created a meaningful evaluation window. The specifics aren't public, but the opportunity is real and time-bound. Engineering teams that sign in H1 2026 lock in for 2–3 years.
Product Engineer Bet

Three Bets to Win the Product Engineer

Release Validation
  • Guardrail metrics auto-attached to every flag — define KPIs once, they apply to every rollout with experimentation-grade stats.
  • Auto-rollback on guardrail breach — flag causes a regression, Amplitude disables it and alerts via Slack or PagerDuty automatically.
  • Flag → Experiment promotion — one click turns a percentage rollout into a measured A/B test.
Error Monitoring with User Impact
  • Production health ranked by user impact — an exception affecting 3,000 checkout users ranks above one firing 10,000 times but hitting 12.
  • Sentry fires on every exception. Amplitude shows which ones actually matter — because only Amplitude has the behavioral layer.
  • AI-named, severity-scored issues with reproducible steps and one-click session replay.
User-Level Debugging
  • Single timeline — behavioral events, session replay, and error signals in one view. No manual stitching across Datadog, Sentry, and your flag tool.
  • Error-focused filtering and click-to-replay at any moment in the timeline.
  • Natural language search — "show me the moment the user hit a 500 error on checkout" — no event scanning required.
