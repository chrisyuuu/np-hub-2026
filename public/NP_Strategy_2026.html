New Products serves two distinct buyers with different needs, different buying criteria, and different success metrics. This is where we are today, where we need to be by end of 2026, and how we plan to get there.

Current Gap
Today, …
New Products serves two distinct "builder" personas with one blended narrative — and both are under-penetrated.
The first is the product engineer persona; the second is the PM/Marketer/Growth persona. Engineers buy on SDK quality, monitoring/debugging capabilities, and release management. PMs and marketers buy on insight speed, no-code leverage, and ability to act without a ticket.
The product engineer drives the most adoption but has no explicit motion.
Engineers create 53% of Feature Experiments, sign off on feature flags in most deals, and represent a significant share of SR prospects. Yet Feature Experiment attach sits at 18.9% despite $33.8M ARR and 34% YoY growth. We have no dedicated engineering GTM motion and no narrative that speaks to how engineers actually buy.
The PM/Marketer/Growth builder has five products that need a more connected loop.
SR at $9.6M (Zoning expected to close the gap in Q2+, once past the May GA). Web XP at $5M ARR, growing well but still early. Guides & Surveys at $12.8M ARR, steady. AI Feedback at 4% source connection rate — the product exists, the activation funnel needs work. AI Assistant launches April 7. Orchestration, the connective tissue for this persona, releases Q3. The products are solid individually; the opportunity is in routing signal between them.
Enterprise expansion forces the data-team conversation and leaves Amplitude without a clear champion.
Without an engineering motion, every large contract eventually routes through warehouse-native incumbents. Engineering buyers route around the data team, carry larger budgets, use flags, monitor and triage issues on a daily basis — creating multiple high-frequency use cases that customers look to us to solve.

Product Engineer Bet H1 2026 · Critical Window

Winning the Product Engineer

The Target
The product engineer — not DevOps, not the SRE
The person who owns a feature end-to-end: spec → flag → experiment → outcome. Their primary output is the code. They evaluate tools on SDK quality, time-to-first-flag, stats rigor, and whether results are interpretable without a data scientist.
The Wedge
Feature Flags is the entry product. Analytics is the unfair advantage.
An engineer installs the SDK to control a rollout — no analytics relationship required, no sales conversation. From there, Feature Experiment follows naturally: "want to measure this?" No separate measurement system needed because Amplitude is already there.
The Timing
A strategic shift in the market is putting engineers in active re-evaluation — right now.
Recent consolidation among the leading independent flag and experimentation platforms has created a meaningful evaluation window. The specifics aren't public, but the opportunity is real and time-bound. Engineering teams that sign in H1 2026 lock in for 2–3 years.
Three bets to win the product engineer
Release Validation
  • Guardrail metrics auto-attached to every flag — define KPIs once, they apply to every rollout with experimentation-grade stats.
  • Auto-rollback on guardrail breach — flag causes a regression, Amplitude disables it and alerts via Slack or PagerDuty automatically.
  • Flag → Experiment promotion — one click turns a percentage rollout into a measured A/B test.
Error Monitoring with User Impact
  • Production health ranked by user impact — an exception affecting 3,000 checkout users ranks above one firing 10,000 times but affecting 12.
  • Sentry fires on every exception. Amplitude shows which ones actually matter — because only Amplitude has the behavioral layer.
  • AI-named, severity-scored issues with reproducible steps and one-click session replay.
User-Level Debugging
  • Single timeline — behavioral events, session replay, and error signals in one view. No manual stitching across Datadog, Sentry, and your flag tool.
  • Error-focused filtering and click-to-replay at any moment in the timeline.
  • Natural language search — "show me the moment the user hit a 500 error on checkout" — no event scanning required.
